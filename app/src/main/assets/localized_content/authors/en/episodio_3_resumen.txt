Episode 3 Recap: From Old Me to New Me

This episode delves into the most difficult point of the process: the transition. Dispenza describes the change as a crossover between two identities, where the old loses dominance and the new is not yet stabilized. That stretch produces uncertainty and discomfort, and he insists that this discomfort is a normal part of the process.

His “river” metaphor summarizes the chapter: staying on the familiar shore maintains the same life; crossing requires going through the unknown. When the person enters that junction, the conditioned body attempts to regain its familiar chemistry and influences the mind with doubts, excuses, and procrastination.

A very practical lesson appears here: these thoughts of abandonment should not be taken as truth, but as a sign that the previous program is losing territory. For Dispenza, the work is to recognize that resistance and not return to the automatic pattern.

The episode defines change as a progressive uninstallation of the old state of being: less repetition of the same thoughts, less execution of the same behaviors, less dependence on the same emotions. At the same time, it proposes building the new self with intention, rehearsal and emotion.

He also insists on reversing classical logic: not waiting for success, health or relationships to feel high emotions; train the emotion first to condition the body towards that future. In their framework, this changes the signal that the person emits and the way they live their day.

A key part is the use of meditation as a double tool: first to make the old program conscious; then to install the new one. The criterion for progress is not “feeling perfect,” but rather becoming less and less unconscious throughout the day.

Precise technique extracted from the episode

Deconstruction and reconstruction exercise:

1. Deconstruction: listing thoughts, behaviors and emotions of the old self.
2. Reconstruction: defining thoughts, actions and emotions of the new self.
3. Daily mental rehearsal: internally practice how you will act during the day.
4. Behavioral application: when you open your eyes, execute the new pattern in real situations.
5. Active self-observation: detect each return to the old pattern and correct in the moment.

Key practical principle

The change is consolidated when the body stops being the “master” of the old program and once again responds to sustained conscious direction.
