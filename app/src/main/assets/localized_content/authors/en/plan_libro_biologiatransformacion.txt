Practice the ideas from the book: The Biology of Transformation

🟠 On an individual level: reprogram your “internal environment”

The book insists that your biology responds to your perception. In practice, this means working on how you interpret reality.

🔹 A. ​​Detect automatic beliefs

Start by observing patterns like:

* “There is not enough”
* “I have to compete”
* “If I don't control, I lose”

👉 Practical example:
In investment (very aligned with you), go from:

* “The market is a war”
    to:
* “The market is a complex system where I can position myself with an advantage”

That changes decisions, risk and stress.



🔹 B. Change physiological state (real key)

It is not enough to think positively: you have to change the state of the body.

Useful practices:

* Slow breathing (reduces “protection” mode)
* Physical exercise
* Exposure to nature
* Meditation or attention focus

👉 Example:
Before making important decisions (trading, work):

* 5 min breathing → reduce cortisol → more rational decisions

🔹 C. Train perception (not reality, but how you see it)

Make this change:

Situation Automatic interpretation Conscious interpretation
Crisis danger opportunity for readjustment
Error failure feedback
Uncertainty threatens optionality

👉 This does have a neurobiological basis: it changes activation of the nervous system.



🟠 At the environmental level: design cooperative systems

The book insists that the change is not just internal.

🔹 A. ​​Create environments where not everything is competition

Real examples:

* Teams where knowledge is shared (not hidden)
* Open source projects
* Professional collaboration networks

👉 Practical example:
In software development:

* Go from “everyone optimizes their code”
    to “we improve the entire system”



🔹 B. Choose the environment well (this is applied epigenetics)

Your “expression” depends on the environment:

* People with scarcity mentality → they drag you down
* Constructive environments → amplify growth

👉 Rule of thumb:

Don't try to change your biology by fighting the environment, change the environment.



🟠 At the system level: think as a “cell within an organism”

Here is the most powerful part of the book.

🔹 A. ​​Think about systemic impact

Before making a decision, ask yourself:

*Does this only benefit me?
* Or does the system where I participate improve?

👉 Example:

* Company that optimizes short-term profit → damages the system
* Company that creates sustainable value → strengthens the system



🔹 B. Adopt network logic (non-hierarchical)

Direct application:

* Delegate
* Share information
* Create horizontal structures

👉 Example:
Modern startups vs rigid corporations.



🔹 C. Microactions that scale

The book proposes that change is emergent.

👉 Real example:

* 1 person changes habits → influences 5
* 5 influence 20
    → network effect

It is not theory: it is dynamics of complex systems.



🟠Practical protocol (actionable summary)

I leave you something very specific to apply from today:

🧩 Daily routine (10–15 min)

1. Mental check
    *Am I in stress or growth mode?
2. Physiological regulation
    * 5 min breathing or walking
3. Reframe
    * Consciously choose a useful interpretation



🧩 Weekly routine

* Review decisions made from fear vs. clarity
* Adjust environment (people, inputs, habits)



🧩 Key book rule applied

“Don't fight the system → change the conditions of the system”



⚠️ Important realism

To avoid falling into the most “idealistic” part of the book:

* ✔️ Yes: environment + perception influence a lot
* ❌ No: you don't control everything with your mind
* ✔️ Yes: you can increase the odds in your favor



🟠 Clear conclusion

Applying this book is not about “thinking pretty”, but about:

* Regulate your physiological state
* Redesign your environment
* Make decisions with systemic logic

That does produce real changes.
