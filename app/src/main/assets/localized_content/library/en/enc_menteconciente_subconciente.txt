The conscious and subconscious mind: a bridge between science and consciousness

From neuroscience, the human mind is divided into two large layers: the conscious and the subconscious. The conscious mind is like the driver of a car: it makes decisions, analyzes information and plans actions. It represents approximately 10% of our mental activity, the part that we feel we “control.” On the other hand, the subconscious mind functions as the engine: it stores our memories, habits, beliefs and emotions, and automatically controls the remaining 90% of our daily life, including physical reactions, behavioral patterns and health. Modern science has shown that brain plasticity allows these two layers to interact: our thoughts and emotions can literally rewire our brain and body.

Authors such as Neville Goddard, Joe Dispenza, Gregg Braden, and Bruce Lipton have explored this connection from different but complementary perspectives. Goddard tells us that imagination is the door to the subconscious mind: what we imagine with intense feeling is recorded inside us and shapes our reality. Dispenza, from applied neuroscience, explains how meditation and focused attention can reprogram neural patterns and release old beliefs that limit our lives. Gregg Braden highlights the importance of coherence between mind and heart, showing that our emotions can influence the energy field that surrounds us and, therefore, our experiences. Finally, Bruce Lipton demonstrates with his cellular studies that subconscious beliefs directly impact genetic expression, meaning that our biology responds to what we “believe” at a deep level.

Although each author has their own particular approach, they all agree on something crucial: the subconscious mind has immense power and can be reprogrammed with intention, emotion, and conscious repetition. Consciousness alone is not enough; It is the bridge between the reality we live and the reality we can create.

Practical exercise to access the subconscious mind
    1. Find a quiet place where you won't be interrupted for 10 minutes.
    2. Breathe deeply 5 times, inhaling through your nose and exhaling through your mouth, letting your body relax.
    3. Visualize a desire or goal as if it were already reality. For example: imagine that you have the job you want, feel the joy and gratitude as if you have already achieved it.
    4. Feel the intense emotion: The key is not just to imagine, but to deeply feel that what you want is already here.
    5. Mentally repeat a short affirmation that reflects your goal, such as “I am in harmony with my success” or “My body and mind work in perfect synchrony.”
    6. Let your mind relax before opening your eyes. Do this daily for 5 to 10 minutes. Constant repetition sends a clear message to the subconscious mind, reprogramming habits and limiting beliefs.

This simple exercise combines Goddard's visualization, Braden's mind-body coherence, Dispenza's neural reprogramming and Lipton's biology, allowing the subconscious mind to become an ally of your goals and well-being.
