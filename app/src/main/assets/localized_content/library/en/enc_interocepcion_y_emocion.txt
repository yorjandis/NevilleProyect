Interoception and emotions

Interoception is the perception of the body's internal signals: heart rate, breathing, muscle tension, temperature, digestion, etc.
    • These signals come from organs, muscles, blood vessels and nervous systems such as the enteric or the intrinsic heart.
    • The brain (mainly the insula, somatosensory cortex, and prefrontal cortex) receives this information and continuously integrates it.

This integration provides the brain with a dynamic “map” of the internal state of the body, on which the emotional experience is built.



2️⃣ Cognitive interpretation of the body state
    • The brain does not receive ready emotions, but rather physical and chemical signals: adrenaline, cortisol, muscle tension, etc.
    • Conscious emotion arises when the brain interprets these signals within a context.
    • Example: increased heart rate
    • Context = job interview → interpretation = anxiety or nervousness
    • Context = sports match → interpretation = excitement or enthusiasm
    • This explains why the same physiological activation can produce different emotions depending on the situation.



3️⃣ Body context and emotional response
    • The “body context” includes all the physiological signals that indicate how the body is at this moment:
    • State of sympathetic/parasympathetic activation
    • Circulating hormones
    • Muscular and visceral sensations
    • Emotion is the interpretation of these signals: it is not simply the chemical signal, but the reading that the brain makes of them in relation to the situation.

Complete example:
    • You are walking and your heart races:
    1. Body signal → high heart rate, rapid breathing.
    2. Interoception → the brain perceives these internal signals.
    3. Context → you hear a roar behind you.
    4. Interpretation → “I'm afraid!” → the conscious emotion of fear arises.

If instead of a roar you were running towards a goal, the same heart rate would be interpreted as enthusiasm or excitement, not fear.



Conclusion
    • The emotion is emergent, not simply chemical nor just cerebral: it arises from the cognitive interpretation of the internal state of the body.
    • Interoception is the key, because it allows the brain to “read” how the body is at all times.
    • The bodily and external context determines how that emotion is labeled and experienced.
