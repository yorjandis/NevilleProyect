Practical, structured and physiologically coherent protocol to normalize the circadian rhythm from the molecular basis (melatonin, cortisol, peripheral clock genes, insulin sensitivity and body temperature).

We will divide it into:
    1. Biological principles
    2. Detailed daily protocol
    3. Adjustments according to chronotype
    4. 14-day reset phase
    5. Common mistakes



🟢 Biological principles that we are going to use

The circadian rhythm is mainly regulated by:
    • Light (melanopsin → suprachiasmatic nucleus)
    • Darkness (melatonin)
    • Morning cortisol
    • Feeding rhythm (liver)
    • Body temperature
    • Movement

The key is not to “sleep more”, but to increase circadian amplitude (clarity between day and night signal).



🟢 Optimal Daily Program

Assuming target schedule:
Sleep 23:00 – 7:00

(Later I'll explain how to adapt it.)



🔶 1. Within the first 10–20 minutes after waking up

Objective: Trigger natural cortisol and suppress residual melatonin.

✔ Go outside for 5–15 minutes (even if it's cloudy).
✔ Without sunglasses.
✔ Looking towards the horizon (not at the direct sun).

Why it works:
    • Activates melanopsin (~480 nm)
    • Resets the suprachiasmatic nucleus
    • Activates PER1/PER2 in peripheral tissues via cortisol

If you can't get out:
    • Artificial light ≥10,000 lux (less ideal but useful)


🔶 2. Early movement (before 9:00)

10–20 min of light walking or mobility.

Effects:
    • Increases body temperature
    • Peak cortisol power
    • Improves peripheral muscle synchronization

Avoid HIIT on an empty stomach if you are very dysregulated.



🔶 3. Coffee strategically

Wait 60–90 minutes after waking up.

Reason:
You avoid interfering with the natural peak of cortisol and adenosine.



🔶 4. First meal within 60–90 min after waking up

Important for the liver.

Ideal:
    • Protein ≥30 g
    • Healthy fats
    • Moderate carbohydrates

Example:
Eggs + avocado + fruit or Greek yogurt + red fruits + nuts.

This:
    • Activates physiological insulin
    • Synchronizes liver clock
    • Activates mTOR early (where it should be)



🔶 5. Second sun exposure (noon)

10–20 minutes.

Reinforces:
    • Circadian amplitude
    • Serotonin production
    • Mood stability



🔶 6. Ideal training: 15:00–18:00

Here:
    • Body temperature is higher
    • Optimal neuromuscular performance
    • Better glycemic tolerance

Avoid intense training after 8:00 p.m. if you are out of sync.



🔶 7. Sunset: transition signal

Go outside for 5–10 minutes in low natural light.

The reddish spectrum:
    • Reduces melanopsin activation
    • Facilitates progressive increase in melatonin



🔶 8. 2–3 hours before bed

Key rules:

✔ Dim lights
✔ Avoid intense blue light
✔ No bright screens without filter
✔ Light dinner

Ideal dinner:
    • Moderate protein
    • Vegetables
    • Low in refined carbohydrates

Avoid:
    • Alcohol
    • Very fatty foods
    • High glycemic loads



🔶 9. 60 minutes before bed

Fixed routine:
    • Warm light
    • Reading on paper
    • Slow breathing (4-6 cycles/min)
    • Room temperature 18–20°C

The thermal drop facilitates melatonin.



🟢 14 Day Reset Protocol (if you are very upset)

For 2 weeks:
    1. Fixed wake-up time (even weekends)
    2. Mandatory sun exposure tomorrow
    3. No naps >20 min
    4. Zero screens 90 minutes before bed
    5. Last meal 3 hours before bedtime
    6. Alcohol-free

The body needs consistent repetition to reset molecular phase.



🟢 Adjustment according to chronotype

If you are very nocturnal:
    • Wake up 15 minutes earlier every 3 days.
    • Mandatory immediate sun exposure.
    • Very early dinner.

If you are a very early riser:
    • Keep bright light in the evening (until 7:00 p.m.).
    • Dinner slightly later.
    • Avoid going to bed excessively early.



🟢 Signs that the protocol works

✔ You wake up before the alarm
✔ You are really hungry in the morning
✔ Peak energy 9:00–12:00
✔ Natural drowsiness 22:00–23:00
✔ Deep sleep in first 90 min

🟢 Mistakes that destroy rhythm
    • Intense night light
    • Eat at midnight
    • Sleep late on weekends
    • Train intensely at 10:00 p.m.
    • Look at your cell phone in bed
