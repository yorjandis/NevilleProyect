Stress hormones are chemicals that the body releases when it perceives a threat, pressure or challenge, whether physical or psychological. Its main function is to prepare us to react quickly, which is known as the “fight or flight” response.

In the short term, these hormones are essential for survival and performance.

The human body does not distinguish between a real threat and a psychological one.
Therefore, modern stress activates the same hormones as physical danger, but continuously, which ends up affecting metabolism, brain, sex hormones and longevity.


✔ Specific stress → improves performance
❌ Constant stress → damages health

The problem appears when the body never “turns off” this response.

🟢 How to reduce the impact of stress hormones and related hormones
Some practical strategies:
    • 💤 Sleep well (7–9 hours)
    • 🚶‍♂️ Moderate physical activity
    • 🧘 Deep breathing and meditation
    • 🥗 Natural and stable glucose diet
    • 🌞 Exposure to sunlight
    • 📵 Reduce constant stimuli (notifications, multitasking)
    
    
🟢 Quick Sort

Below is a list of the main stress hormones.
    
🟠 Cortisol

Glucocorticoid – adrenal cortex

Effects:
    • Increases blood glucose
    • Mobilizes fats and proteins
    • Reduces inflammation and immunity
    • Affects memory, mood and sleep

Triggers:
    • Prolonged psychological stress
    • Lack of sleep
    • Hypoglycemia
    • Infections
    • Prolonged intense exercise


🟠Adrenaline (epinephrine)

Catecholamine – adrenal medulla

Effects:
    • Increases heart rate and blood pressure
    • Dilates bronchi
    • Releases glucose quickly
    • Improves strength and reaction speed

Triggers:
    • Fear
    • Immediate danger
    • Surprise
    • Explosive exercise


🟠 Norepinephrine (norepinephrine)

Catecholamine – adrenal medulla and nervous system

Effects:
    • Increases alertness and concentration
    • Vasoconstriction
    • Maintains attention under pressure

Triggers:
    • Mental stress
    • Cold
    • Hypotension
    • Prolonged surveillance


🟠CRH (Corticotropin Releasing Hormone)

hypothalamus

Effects:
    • Starts the stress cascade
    • Stimulates ACTH release
    • Increases anxiety

Triggers:
    • Threat perception
    • Emotional stress
    • Circadian rhythm (morning)


🟠 ACTH (Adrenocorticotropic hormone)

anterior pituitary

Effects:
    • Stimulates cortisol production
    • Increases response to prolonged stress

Triggers:
    • Release of CRH
    • Sustained stress


🟠Vasopressin (ADH)

Hypothalamus/posterior pituitary

Effects:
    • Water retention
    • Increases blood pressure
    • Enhances the release of ACTH

Triggers:
    • Intense stress
    • Dehydration
    • Hypotension


🟠 Aldosterone

Mineralocorticoid – adrenal cortex

Effects:
    • Sodium retention
    • Increased blood volume
    • Raises blood pressure

Triggers:
    • Physical stress
    • Fluid loss
    • Activation of the renin-angiotensin system


🟠 Glucagon

Pancreas

Effects:
    • Increases blood glucose
    • Stimulates hepatic energy release

Triggers:
    • Fasting
    • Metabolic stress
    • Intense exercise

🟠 Growth hormone (GH)

anterior pituitary

Effects:
    • Mobilizes fats
    • Reduces glucose use
    • Protects muscle mass in stress

Triggers:
    • Physical stress
    • Fasting
    • Exercise
    • Deep sleep

🟠 Prolactin

anterior pituitary

Effects:
    • Modulates immune system
    • Increases in emotional stress
    • Can inhibit sex hormones if chronically elevated

Triggers:
    • Psychological stress
    • Lack of sleep
    • Pain

🟠 β-endorphins

Pituitary and nervous system

Effects:
    • Natural analgesia
    • Feeling of relief or euphoria
    • Reduces pain perception

Triggers:
    • Extreme stress
    • Pain
    • Intense exercise


🟠 Dopamine (related to adaptive stress)

nervous system

Effects:
    • Increases motivation
    • Improves short-term focus
    • In excess: anxiety

Triggers:
    • Anticipatory stress
    • Challenges
    • Expectation of reward
