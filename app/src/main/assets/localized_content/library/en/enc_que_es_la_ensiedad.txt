Anxiety, seen from neuroscience, is not a “defect” or a weakness: it is an evolutionary survival mechanism that becomes problematic when it is activated too intensely, frequently or without a real threat.

I go in parts and then we go down to practical routines.



🟢 What is anxiety in the brain?

The central circuit

Anxiety arises from the interaction between several brain structures:

🔸Amygdala (alarm)
    • Detects threats (real or imagined).
    • It works quickly and unconsciously.
    • When hyperactive → generates fear, tension, hypervigilance.

👉 In chronic anxiety, the amygdala becomes hypersensitive.


🔸Prefrontal cortex (control)
    • Rational part: analyzes, decides, regulates emotions.
    • “Tells” the amygdala whether there is real danger or not.

👉 In anxiety:
    • The amygdala dominates
    • The prefrontal cortex is partially disconnected

Therefore:

“I know it's not serious... but my body doesn't understand it”


🔸Hippocampus (emotional memory)
    • Contextualize past experiences.
    • If there was repeated stress, learn that “the world is dangerous.”

👉 The brain learns anxiety by repetition.



🔸Autonomous nervous system

Fight/flight mode is activated:
    • ↑ cortisol
    • ↑ adrenaline
    • ↑ heart rate
    • ↓ digestion
    • ↑ muscle tension

Anxiety is, literally, a body prepared to survive... when it is not necessary.



🟢 Why does it become chronic?

From neuroscience, anxiety is maintained by:

Feedback loops
    1. Anticipatory thinking (“what if it happens…?”)
    2. Body activation
    3. Catastrophic interpretation of sensations
    4. More activation

👉 The brain confuses internal sensations with external danger.

⸻

🟢 Neuronal plasticity
    • Neurons that activate together, connect together.
    • The more anxious you are, the easier it is to become anxious.

The good news: this also works in favor of reversing it.



🟢 Effective routines to control anxiety (based on neuroscience)

They are not “tips”, they are direct interventions on the brain.



🔸Slow and controlled breathing (the most powerful)

Why it works:
    • Activates the vagus nerve
    • Deactivates the amygdala
    • Lowers cortisol in minutes

Practical routine:

Breathing 4–6
    • Inhale 4 seconds
    • Exhale 6 seconds
    • 5–10 minutes

👉 Long exhalation is the key.

daily example
    • When you wake up
    • Before sleeping
    • In anxiety peaks


🔸Rhythmic physical movement

Why it works:
    • Reduces cortisol
    • Increases BDNF (neuroplasticity)
    • “Discharge” accumulated adrenaline

Best options:
    • Walk 30–45 min
    • Run easy
    • Bicycle
    • Swim

👉 It doesn't have to be intense. Rhythmic > explosive.


🔸Attention training (meditation)

It is not “blank your mind.”

What it does in the brain:
    • Strengthens prefrontal cortex
    • Reduces functional volume of the amygdala
    • Improves emotional control

Simple routine:
    • 10 minutes
    • Attention to breathing
    • When the mind leaves → you come back without a fight

realistic example
    • 5 min tomorrow
    • 5 min late



🔸Regulatory writing

Why it works:
    • Externalize thoughts
    • Reduces rumination
    • Organizes prefrontal networks

Exercise:

For 10 minutes write:
    • What worries me
    • What is real
    • What is anticipation

👉 The brain needs to get the threat out of the head onto paper.



🔸Circadian rhythms (fundamental)

Neurobiological impact:
    • Irregular sleep = overreactive amygdala
    • Sleep well = better emotional regulation

Base routine:
    • Natural sun in the morning (5–10 min)
    • Sleep and wake up at the same time
    • Avoid screens 90 minutes before bed



🔸Nutrition and anxiety (silent key)

From neuroscience:
    • 90% of serotonin is produced in the intestine
    • Inflammation = anxiety

Prioritize:
    • Sufficient protein
    • Omega 3
    • Real food

Reduces:
    • Sugar
    • Ultra-processed
    • Excess caffeine



🟢 An important idea to integrate

Anxiety is not “eliminated”, the nervous system is re-educated

Each routine is a repeated message to the brain:

"There is no danger. You can relax."

With constancy:
    • The amygdala calms down
    • The prefrontal cortex regains control
    • The body stops living on alert
