Meditation: The Door to Create Our Reality

Meditation is much more than sitting in silence; It is a space where our mind and body synchronize, where the outside noise fades away and we begin to connect with our inner world. In simple words, meditating is training our attention, learning to observe our thoughts without getting carried away by them and opening ourselves to the possibility of transforming our life experience.

Authors such as Neville Goddard, Joe Dispenza, Gregg Braden and Bruce Lipton agree on a crucial point: meditation is the tool that allows us to access the part of our mind capable of creating the reality we desire. Goddard tells us about imagining with intensity and emotion the future we want; According to him, our external reality reflects our most sustained internal images. Dispenza, from neuroscience, explains that each repeated thought generates neural connections that configure our biology, and meditating allows us to reprogram these connections towards new possibilities. Braden contributes that our emotions and beliefs influence the energy fields around us, and meditating helps us align with those energies to manifest positive changes. Lipton, from cell biology, states that our beliefs can “turn on” or “turn off” genes, and meditation is a way of sending conscious signals to our body and mind to create well-being and health.

Together, these teachings show us that meditation is not just relaxation: it is training the mind to direct attention and emotion towards what we want to experience, influencing both our brain, our body and our environment. It is like planting a seed in fertile soil: the seed is the idea or intention, the emotion and consciousness are the water and the sun, and meditation is the act of caring for the soil so that that seed grows.

Practical exercise to enter meditation:
    1. Find a quiet place and sit comfortably.
    2. Close your eyes and take three deep breaths, inhaling through your nose and exhaling through your mouth.
    3. Visualize a scene that represents the reality you desire: it can be a moment of success, health, joy or peace. Don't just see it, feel it: emotions like gratitude, pride or happiness make the image more real.
    4. If your mind wanders, gently return your attention to your breathing and the sensation of your visualization.
    5. Spend at least 5 minutes on this practice, increasing the time as you feel comfortable.

With consistency, this simple habit allows our mind and body to align with the possibilities we desire, demonstrating that creating our reality is not magic: it is the science of training consciousness.
