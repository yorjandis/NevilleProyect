Dopamine is often oversimplified (“the pleasure hormone”), but from neuroscience its role is much more interesting and useful for understanding behavior, motivation and habits.



🟢 What is dopamine from neuroscience?

Dopamine is a catecholaminergic neurotransmitter that acts as a modulating signal, not as a simple “switch” of pleasure.

Rather than generating pleasure, dopamine signals relevance and prediction of reward. It tells the brain:

“This is important, pay attention to it and repeat it if it works.”

It is mainly produced in:
    • Ventral tegmental area (VTA)
    • Substantia nigra

And it acts on key circuits:
    • Reward system
    • Reinforcement learning
    • Motor control
    • Executive functions


🟢 Dopamine ≠ pleasure (key clarification)

A common mistake is to think that:
    • Dopamine = pleasure

Actually:
    • Dopamine → desire, motivation, anticipation
    • Endogenous opioids and serotonin → pleasure and well-being

Practical example:
    • Wanting to check your cell phone 📱 → dopamine
    • Feeling satisfied after achieving something → other systems

That's why you can want something that doesn't even make you feel good afterwards.



🟢 How does it modulate behavior?

🔸A. Reward prediction signal

Dopamine is not released so much when you get the reward, but when:
    • You anticipate it
    • Something is better than expected
    • Something is new or uncertain

Example:
    • First coffee of the day ☕ → high dopamine
    • Coffee number 10 → almost nothing

The brain learns from prediction errors:
    • If something exceeds expectations → dopamine ↑
    • If you disappoint → dopamine ↓

This is the basis of behavioral learning.



🔸B. Attentional focus and behavioral energy

Dopamine also regulates:
    • Ability to concentrate
    • Initiative
    • Persistence in tasks

Low levels:
    • Apathy
    • Lack of motivation
    • Difficulty initiating actions

Poorly regulated high levels:
    • Impulsivity
    • Compulsive search for stimuli

⸻

🟢 Dopamine and habit formation

A habit is formed when the brain learns:

“This action → predictable result”

Dopamine does not reinforce the result, but:
    • The circuit that led to it

Typical cycle:
    1. Signal (boredom)
    2. Behavior (infinite scroll)
    3. Mini reward (new)
    4. Dopamine strengthens the circuit

Over time:
    • The initial stimulus already triggers dopamine
    • Behavior becomes automatic

Therefore:
    • Social networks, ultra-processed food and gambling are so addictive
    • They are designed to maximize intermittent dopamine



🟢 What happens when dopamine is deregulated

🔸Chronic excess stimulation

Examples:
    • Constant scroll
    • Pornography
    • Sugar + fat + salt
    • Games of chance

Neurobiological consequence:
    • ↓ sensitivity of dopaminergic receptors
    • Need for increasingly intense stimuli
    • Anhedonia (nothing motivates)

Example:

Nothing interests you, but you continue looking for stimuli.



🔸Functional dopaminergic deficit

It can be seen at:
    • Depression
    • Burnout
    • Parkinson's (extreme case)

Symptoms:
    • Lack of initiative
    • Procrastination
    • Feeling of “I don't care about anything”



🟢 Healthy way to maintain optimal dopamine levels

Here's what's really important 👇

🔸A. Regulate dopamine, not maximize it

The key is not to “have more dopamine”, but to: Maintain the sensitivity of the system


🔸B. Neuroscience-backed strategies

👉1. Effort before reward
Dopamine increases during directed effort.

Example:
    • Train before music or stimuli
    • Work for 90 minutes and then rest

Avoid:
    • Immediate reward without effort



👉2. Physical exercise (especially aerobic and strength)
Effects:
    • ↑ basal dopamine
    • ↑ dopaminergic receptors
    • ↑ BDNF (brain plasticity)

Practical example:
    • Walk 30–45 min daily
    • Strength training 2–3 times/week



👉3. Deep and regular sleep
During sleep:
    • Dopaminergic receptors are restored
    • Motivation is recalibrated

Sleep little:
    • Reduces functional dopamine
    • Impulsive search increases

👉4. Controlled exposure to discomfort
Examples:
    • Cold showers
    • Intermittent fasting (well done)
    • Difficult tasks without distractions

This increases:
    • Tonic dopamine (baseline)
    • Motivational resilience



👉5. Artificial peak reduction
Limit:
    • Constant social networks
    • Fast and frequent stimuli
    • Multitasking

Rule of thumb:

Less stimuli → more natural motivation


🔸C. Nutrition and dopamine

Dopamine is synthesized from tyrosine.

Useful foods:
    • Eggs
    • Fish
    • Legumes
    • Nuts
    • Banana
    • Dark chocolate (moderate)

Key Micronutrients:
    • Iron
    • Zinc
    • Vitamin B6

Avoid:
    • Constant sugar spikes
    • Ultra-processed


🟢 Key idea to close

Dopamine doesn't make you happy.
It pushes you to act.

If you use it wrong:
    • You become a slave to stimulation

If you regulate it well:
    • You build discipline, solid habits and purpose


In summary:
A life with fewer easy stimuli produces more real motivation.
