In neuroscience, the “energy savings” associated with the formation of habits is not a metaphor, but a measurable phenomenon at the metabolic, synaptic, and neural network organization levels. When a behavior is repeated, the brain changes which circuits are activated, how much energy they consume, and how information is transmitted. Let's go in parts.



🟢 1. What does “saving energy” in the brain really mean?

The brain consumes about 20–25% of the body's total energy expenditure, although it represents only ~2% of body weight.
Most of this expense is due to:
    • Action potentials
    • Release of neurotransmitters
    • Ionic reuptake (Na⁺/K⁺ pumps)
    • Maintenance of active synapses

When an action is new, the brain:
    • Recruit many areas at once
    • Fires neurons with high frequency
    • Uses inefficient and redundant circuits

With repetition, the system reorganizes itself to do the same thing with less neural activation.

👉 Energy savings = fewer active neurons + lower firing rate + shorter and more specific routes.



🟢 2. Changes at the circuit level: from the cortex to the habitual system

Initial phase (new behavior)

The activity predominates in:
    • Prefrontal cortex (conscious control, planning)
    • Anterior cingulate cortex (error monitoring)
    • Hippocampus (explicit memory)

These areas are:
    • Highly energy demanding
    • Slow
    • Designed for flexible decisions

Habitual phase (automated behavior)

Control passes to:
    • Basal ganglia, especially the dorsal striatum
    • Sensorimotor circuits → response

These structures:
    • They work in “automatic mode”
    • They use fewer neurons for the same behavioral output
    • Require less conscious supervision

📌 Result: the prefrontal cortex partially “turns off” for that task.



🟢 3. Energy savings at the synaptic level

a) Selective enhancement and pruning

During repetition:
    • Useful synapses are strengthened (LTP)
    • Irrelevant synapses are weakened or eliminated (synaptic pruning)

This implies:
    • Fewer active synapses → less neurotransmitter release
    • Less vesicular recycling → lower ATP consumption

👉 The brain eliminates “secondary paths” and keeps the cheapest route energetically.

Example

Learn to drive:
    • At the beginning: hundreds of conscious micro-decisions
    • With habit: compact sequence of neuronal activation



🟢 4. Decrease in neuronal firing (firing rate)

Studies with fMRI and electrophysiology show that, after automation:
    • Decreases the BOLD signal (oxygen consumption)
    • Lowers the frequency of action potentials
    • Increases the temporal precision of shots

🧠 Same behavior → less “neural noise” → less energy expenditure

This is crucial because:
    • Each action potential costs ATP
    • The Na⁺/K⁺ pump is one of the largest consumers of energy in the brain



🟢 5. Myelination and speed: less expense due to efficiency

The repetition of specific circuits induces:
    • Increased myelin in frequently used axons

Effects:
    • Faster signals
    • Less electrical loss
    • Less need for repeated firing to ensure transmission

📌 Myelin is like insulating an electric cable: same information, lower cost.



🟢 6. Dopamine: from reward to predictive signal

At the beginning of a behavior:
    • Dopamine is released after reward

With the habit:
    • Dopamine is released earlier, before the signal that predicts the action

This allows:
    • Initiate the behavior without conscious evaluation
    • Reduce the burden of results processing

👉 The brain no longer “evaluates”: it executes.



🟢 7. Why the brain looks for habits (evolutionary principle)

From an adaptive point of view:
    • Energy is limited
    • Automating what is frequent frees up resources to:
    • New
    • Threats
    • Creativity
    • Learning

📌 A brain that does not automate is metabolically exhausted.



🟢 8. Complete practical example: brushing your teeth

Beginner (child):
    • High prefrontal activation
    • Constant supervision
    • Cognitive fatigue

Adult with habit:
    • Minimum basal activation
    • You can think about something else
    • Virtually without conscious effort

👉 It's not that you "don't use your brain", it's that you use a cheaper circuit.



🟢 9. Key takeaway idea

A habit is not just a repeated behavior:
It is an energetic reorganization of the brain to do the same thing with the minimum possible metabolic cost.
