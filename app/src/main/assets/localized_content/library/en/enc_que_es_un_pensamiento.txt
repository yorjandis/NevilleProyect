From neuroscience, a thought is not an isolated “thing” nor an abstract entity independent of the brain, but rather a dynamic process that emerges from the coordinated activity of neural networks distributed in different brain regions.



🟢 Neuroscientific definition of thinking

A thought can be defined as:

A transient pattern of neuronal activation that integrates sensory information, memory, emotion and objectives, and that allows reality to be represented, evaluated or simulated.

It does not occur in a single neuron or in a single area of ​​the brain, but in functional networks that are continually activated and deactivated.



What happens in the brain when we think

🟢 1. Activation of neural networks

Thinking implies that groups of neurons activate synchronously. These neurons:
    • They communicate through electrical impulses (action potentials).
    • They release neurotransmitters (glutamate, dopamine, GABA, serotonin, etc.).

Each thought corresponds to a specific configuration of activity in these networks.

Example:
Thinking “I have an important meeting tomorrow” simultaneously activates:
    • Prefrontal cortex → planning and anticipation.
    • Hippocampus → memory of similar experiences.
    • Amygdala → emotional load (stress or motivation).
    
    

🟢 2. Information integration

A thought is not just memory nor just emotion. It is the integration of multiple systems:
Sensory cortex: Information from the environment
Hippocampus: Memories
Amygdala: Emotional Meaning
Prefrontal cortex: Evaluation and decision making
Basal ganglia: Selection of actions

Thought arises when these systems work together.


🟢 Thinking is simulating

From cognitive neuroscience, thinking is largely about simulating scenarios:
    • The brain recreates past situations (memory)
    • Imagine possible futures
    • Evaluate consequences

This occurs even without external stimuli.

📌 Practical example:
When you imagine a future conversation:
    • Language areas are activated.
    • Motor areas are activated as if you were speaking (but inhibited).
    • The brain “rehearses” without executing.



🟢Thoughts as processes, not objects

A key point:
👉 Thoughts are not stored as files, but as synaptic connection patterns.
    • Repetition strengthens connections (synaptic plasticity).
    • Frequent thoughts become more accessible.
    • This explains mental habits and rumination.

📌 Example:
If a person repeats negative thoughts:
    • Circuits between the amygdala and prefrontal cortex are reinforced.
    • The brain learns that “path” as preferred.



🟢 Types of thoughts from neuroscience
    1. Automatic
    • Fast, unconscious
    • Associated with the limbic system
    • Example: “this is dangerous”
    2. Deliberate
    • Slow, aware
    • Dorsolateral prefrontal cortex
    • Example: analyze an investment
    3. Abstracts
    • Use of symbols and concepts
    • Internal language
    • Example: reflect on justice or time



🟢 In summary

From neuroscience, a thought is:
    • Coordinated electrical and chemical activity
    • A distributed, non-localized pattern
    • Dynamic and changing
    • Integrator of memory, emotion and perception
    • Moldable through experience and attention
