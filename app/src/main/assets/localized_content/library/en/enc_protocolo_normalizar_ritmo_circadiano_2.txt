Practical protocol to normalize the circadian rhythm.

Version specifically aimed at high cognitive performance, prioritizing:
    • High circadian amplitude
    • Stable alert peak 9:00–13:00
    • Maximum mental clarity without peaks and dips
    • Optimization of dopamine, acetylcholine and cortisol
    • Glycemic stability
    • Deep restorative sleep (efficient N3 and REM phase)



🟢 Biological foundation of cognitive performance

Mental performance depends mainly on:
    1. Well-timed morning cortisol spike
    2. Stable dopaminergic activation
    3. Correct brain temperature rhythm
    4. Brain insulin sensitivity
    5. Robust nocturnal melatonin production

If we increase circadian amplitude, mental clarity increases.



DAILY PROTOCOL – HIGH PERFORMANCE

Let's assume target time: 23:00 – 7:00



07:00 – Strategic awakening

🟢 Immediate sunlight (10–20 min)

Exterior, without glasses.

This activates melanopsin → synchronizes the central clock in the suprachiasmatic nucleus → optimizes the cortisol spike.

If you do this right, the brain goes into a state of natural physiological alertness.



🟢 Hydration + electrolytes

Water + pinch of sea salt or electrolytes.

The brain needs adequate plasma volume for optimal performance.



🟢 Light movement (10–15 min)

Fast walking or mobility.

Effect:
    • Increases BDNF
    • Raises body temperature
    • Gently activates catecholamines

No strenuous exercise here.



🟢 08:15 – Smart Coffee

Wait 60–90 minutes after waking up.

This way you don't block the natural cortisol peak.

Ideal:
    • Black coffee
    • Or coffee + L-theanine if you are sensitive

Avoid coffee after 1:00 p.m.



🟢 09:00–11:30 – Deep Cognitive Block #1

Neurobiologically optimal window.

Here:
    • Cortisol still elevated
    • Stable dopamine
    • Low adenosine interference

Do here:
    • Complex programming
    • Deep financial analysis
    • Strategic writing
    • Mathematical resolution

Work in blocks of 90 minutes (ultradian).



🟢 11:30 – First meal

High in protein (30–40 g).

Example:
    • Eggs + vegetables + olive oil
    • Or Greek yogurt + berries + nuts

Avoid:
    • Large loads of simple carbohydrates
    • Glycemic peaks

Glycemic stability = cognitive stability.



🟢 13:00 – Light + short walk

5–10 min outside.

Reinforces:
    • Alert status
    • Serotonin
    • Prevents postprandial depression



🟢 14:00–16:00 – Cognitive Block #2

Lower intensity than the first.

Ideal for:
    • Review
    • Design
    • Strategic decisions

If there is a drop:
    • 5 min of deep nasal breathing
    • Or ultra-short nap 15–20 min (before 3:30 p.m.)



🟢 17:00 – Training

Strength or moderate HIIT.

Here:
    • Maximum body temperature
    • Functional testosterone
    • Better neuromuscular performance

Exercise here improves deep sleep.



🟢 19:00 – Light dinner

Protein + vegetables.

Moderate complex carbohydrates if you trained.

Avoid:
    • Alcohol
    • Sugars
    • Caloric excess

Dinner 3 hours before bed.



🟢 21:30 – Cognitive slowdown

Warm lights.

No bright screens.

You can use:
    • Physical reading
    • Diary
    • Slow breathing (4–6 rpm)



🟢 23:00 – Sleep

Room:
    • Total darkness
    • 18–20°C
    • Silent

First deep sleep cycle is key to declarative memory consolidation.



🟢 Additional optimizers (optional)

🔸 Temporary food restriction (10–11 a.m.)

Example: 11:30–21:00

Improvement:
    • Insulin sensitivity
    • Hepatic clock amplitude
    • Stable mental energy



🔸 Exposure to red light at night (very dim)

Reduces melanopsin activation.



🔸 Strategic supplements (if applicable)
    • Magnesium glycinate night
    • Glycine 3 g before bed
    • Creatine 3–5 g/day (proven cognitive benefit)


🟢 Signs of achieved optimization

✔ Sustained mental clarity 9–13

✔ Natural fatigue 22:30

🟢 What destroys cognitive performance the most
    • Sleep late on weekends
    • Intense screens 22:00+
    • Eat late
    • Coffee after 2:00 p.m.
    • Lack of morning light
