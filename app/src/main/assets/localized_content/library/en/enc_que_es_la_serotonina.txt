Serotonin is a neurotransmitter (a chemical that neurons use to communicate) and also acts as a hormone in different parts of the body. It is often called the “feel-good hormone,” but it is actually more accurate to see it as a master regulator: it influences mood, sleep, digestion, and many other processes.



🟢 Where is serotonin produced?

A key fact:
👉 approx. 90–95% of serotonin is produced in the gut, not the brain.
Only a small part is synthesized in the central nervous system.

Main places:
    • Intestine (enteroendocrine cells)
    • Brain (raphe nuclei)
    • Platelets (related to coagulation)


🟢 Main functions of serotonin

🔸Mood and emotional well-being
    • Contributes to a feeling of calm, stability and satisfaction
    • Helps regulate anxiety and irritability
    • Low levels are associated with depression, chronic anxiety and sudden mood swings.

📌 Practical example:
It is not the “euphoria” of dopamine, but the feeling of “everything is in order.”



🔸Sleep regulation
    • It is a precursor of melatonin, the sleep hormone
    • Influences the circadian rhythm (day-night)

📌 Example:
Sleeping poorly for several days reduces serotonin → worse mood → even worse sleep (vicious circle).



🔸Appetite and satiety
    • Helps regulate hunger, especially the desire for carbohydrates
    • Low levels are often associated with sugar cravings

📌 Example:
When someone is stressed and constantly reaching for sweets, there is often low serotonin.



🔸Digestion
    • Regulates intestinal movement
    • Influences visceral sensitivity (abdominal pain)

📌 Example:
Irritable bowel syndrome has a direct relationship with serotonin signaling.



🔸Cognitive function
    • Participates in memory
    • Helps mental flexibility and impulse control


🔸Blood coagulation
    • Platelets use serotonin to help form clots when there is an injury



🟢 What happens when there are imbalances?

low serotonin
    • Persistent sadness
    • Anxiety
    • Sleep problems
    • Sugar cravings
    • Low stress tolerance
    • Digestive problems

Excessive serotonin (rare naturally)
    • It usually occurs due to drugs (serotonin syndrome).
    • Agitation, sweating, tachycardia, confusion
👉 It is not something that happens due to diet or healthy habits



🟢 How to enhance and regulate serotonin in a healthy way?

🔸Food (absolute key)

Serotonin is made from the amino acid tryptophan.

Foods rich in tryptophan:
    • Eggs
    • Turkey and chicken
    • Fish (salmon, sardines)
    • Natural yogurt and kefir
    • Legumes (lentils, chickpeas)
    • Nuts (walnuts, almonds)
    • Seeds (sesame, pumpkin)
    • Banana

📌 Important tip:
Tryptophan enters the brain better when combined with complex carbohydrates.

Practical example:
    • Natural yogurt + oats + nuts
    • Lentils with brown rice


🔸Take care of the intestine (essential)

Since most of the serotonin is produced there:
    • Consume fiber (vegetables, fruits, legumes)
    • Fermented foods: kefir, sauerkraut, natural yogurt
    • Avoid excess ultra-processed foods and alcohol

📌 Example:
A diverse microbiota → better serotonin production → better mood.


🔸Sun exposure
    • Direct sunlight stimulates serotonin production
    • 15–30 min daily (better in the morning)

📌 Example:
Going for a walk without sunglasses early improves mood and sleep that same night.


🔸Regular physical exercise

Especially:
    • Walk
    • Run easy
    • Strength training
    • Yoga

📌 Example:
30 minutes of moderate exercise can elevate serotonin for hours.


🔸Sleep well
    • Little sleep reduces serotonin
    • Sleeping well stabilizes it and allows it to be converted into melatonin

Good practices:
    • Regular hours
    • Avoid screens 1 hour before bedtime
    • Dark and cool bedroom


🔸Stress management

Elevated cortisol blocks serotonin signaling.

Useful tools:
    • Deep breathing
    • Meditation
    • Nature walks
    • Reflective writing

📌 Simple example:
Breathing 4–6 seconds for 5 minutes reduces stress and promotes serotonin.


🔸Social contact and purpose
    • Quality relationships
    • Sense of belonging
    • Activities with meaning

📌 Example:
Deep conversation or helping others raises serotonin more than quick stimuli.


🟢 Quick summary

Serotonin:
    • It's not just “happiness”, it's balance
    • Affects mind, intestine, sleep and appetite
    • It is regulated more by daily habits than by quick solutions

Enhancing it in a healthy way implies:
    • Eat well
    • Sleep better
    • Move the body
    • Take care of the intestine
    • Reduce stress
    • Receive sunlight
    • Connect with others
