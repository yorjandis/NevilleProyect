Practical protocol to reinterpret emotions

Practical 5-step protocol, based on neuroscience, interoception and conscious emotional regulation, to learn to reinterpret your emotions in real time. It is designed for everyday situations such as fear, anxiety, anger or stress, but can be adapted to other emotions.

🟢 Step 1: Recognize the body signal
    • Observe what is happening in your body at this moment: heart rate, breathing, muscle tension, feeling of heat/cold, pressure in the stomach or chest.
    • Don't judge the sensation, just notice its presence.
    • Quick exercise: close your eyes and ask internally:
“What do I feel in my body now?”
    • This step activates interoception, the basis for being able to reinterpret emotion.



🟢 Step 2: Name the emotion
    • Label the emotion you are feeling: fear, anger, sadness, anxiety, excitement.
    • Naming it allows the prefrontal cortex to take control over the amygdala and other limbic centers.
    • Tip: if you are not sure, use a list of basic emotions and choose the closest one.
    • Example: “I am feeling afraid” or “I feel tension and nervousness.”



🟢 Step 3: Analyze the context
    • Ask yourself:
    • “What situation am I experiencing?”
    • “Does this bodily activation mean real danger or is it an automatic reaction?”
    • This step allows us to separate the body signal from the automatic meaning that the brain assigns.
    • Example: racing heart during a presentation → real danger or excitement to concentrate and communicate better.



🟢 Step 4: Reinterpret the emotion
    • Change the narrative you give to body signals.
    • Ask yourself:
    • “How can I see this activation as useful or adaptive?”
    • “What more constructive emotion can I generate from these signals?”
    • Example:
    • Sign: racing heart, sweating.
    • Automatic narrative: “I'm nervous and I'm going to fail.”
    • Conscious reinterpretation: “My body is alert and prepared; I can use this energy to communicate safely.”
    • This reinterpretation activates the prefrontal cortex and reinforces new neural connections, changing the emotion experienced.



🟢 Step 5: Anchor the new emotion
    • Reinforces reinterpretation using body techniques:
    • Deep, controlled breathing (decreases sympathetic activation).
    • Open and relaxed posture (sends signals to the brain of security and trust).
    • Visualization: Imagine the positive outcome of your action or situation.
    • Repeating this practice creates neuroplasticity, making it easier for future bodily activations to be interpreted positively in a more automatic way.



🟢Practical tips
    1. Practice in low intensity situations before applying in stressful situations.
    2. Keep a brief record of each reinterpretation and the resulting effect.
    3. Use short, positive phrases for reinterpretation (“I am ready”, “This is useful energy”).
    4. Combine with mindfulness or meditation to train body awareness and improve interoception.


🟢 Conceptual summary:
    • Emotions arise from the cognitive interpretation of the bodily state.
    • By becoming aware of the body, labeling the emotion, analyzing the context and reinterpreting the signal, we can change the emotion experienced in real time.
    • Constant practice reinforces these new neural pathways, increasing resilience and emotional well-being.
