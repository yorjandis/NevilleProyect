The weakening of a habit depends on the level of consolidation of the circuit, not a magic number of days. A more complete explanation is given below:


🟢 1. Why “21 days” or “66 days” do not make neurobiological sense

Those numbers come from behavioral averages, not direct measurements of:
    • Synaptic potentiation
    • Striatal activity
    • Prefrontal inhibition
    • Anticipatory dopaminergic signals

Two habits can take radically different times, even if they “look” the same from the outside.

📌 The brain doesn't measure days, it measures repetitions with or without reward.



🟢 2. What “weakening a habit” really means in the brain

From neurobiology, a habit is weakened when this happens:
    1. The signal keeps appearing
    2. The impulse appears weaker or shorter
    3. Automatic behavior is not executed
    4. The alternative circuit is activated

That implies:
    • ↓ anticipatory dopamine
    • ↓ dominance of the dorsal striatum
    • ↑ inhibitory control of the prefrontal cortex



🟢 3. Real time phases (based on physiology)

🧠 Phase 1: initial destabilization

3–7 days

What happens:
    • The usual circuit is still active
    • But the reward prediction starts to fail
    • Anticipatory dopamine is not confirmed

Physiology:
    • Onset of synaptic depression (LTD)
    • Increased prefrontal-striatum conflict

Subjective sensation:
    • Discomfort
    • Strong but frustrated impulse
    • Cognitive fatigue

👉 This is the phase where most abandon.



🧠 Phase 2: functional weakening

2–4 weeks

What happens:
    • The impulse appears with less intensity
    • Execution is no longer automatic
    • The brain “doubts”

Physiology:
    • ↓ neuronal firing of the usual circuit
    • ↓ dopaminergic release upon signal
    • Initial strengthening of the alternative circuit

Clear signal:
    • A space appears between stimulus and response

📌 Here the habit is already really weakening.



🧠 Phase 3: stable inhibition

1–3 months

What happens:
    • The old habit still “exists”
    • But it is rarely executed
    • Requires specific stress or context to reappear

Physiology:
    • More efficient prefrontal domain
    • New circuit with lower energy cost
    • Beginning of myelination of the alternative habit

Feeling:
    • Less effort
    • Less temptation
    • More feeling of control



🟢 4. Factors that accelerate or delay the process

🔥 They accelerate
    • Completely eliminate the signal (environment)
    • Clear substitution (not just inhibition)
    • Sleep well
    • Low exposure to stress
    • Immediate reward for the new habit

🧊 They delay
    • Chronic stress
    • Lack of sleep
    • Hunger / hypoglycemia
    • Constant multitasking
    • Frequent “exceptions”

📌 Each exception reactivates the old circuit.



🟢 5. Habits according to your “neural depth”

Superficial

(consult networks, snack on snacks)
    • 1–3 weeks for clear weakening

Intermediates

(coffee + sweets, cell phone when you wake up)
    • 3–6 weeks

Deep

(tobacco, alcohol, emotional escape behaviors)
    • 2–6 months or more

👉 Not for morality, but for dopaminergic density and myelination.



🟢 6. Simple neurobiological rule

A habit begins to weaken
when the signal appears repeatedly without the expected reward,
and there is an alternative that does provide it.

It is not time that changes the brain,
it is the repeated experience of failed prediction.



🟢 7. Objective sign of progress (very important)

If this happens:
    • The impulse appears
    • But it doesn't “drag” you
    • And you can choose without intense struggle

So, at the neuronal level, the habit is already losing strength.



🟢 8. Final idea to integrate

Breaking a habit is not an event,
It is a process of energetic and predictive readjustment of the brain.
