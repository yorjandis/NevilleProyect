From neuroscience and psychoneuroimmunology (PNI), the relationship between thoughts, emotions and the immune system is direct, bidirectional and measurable. It's not an abstract idea: there are well-described neural, endocrine, and immunological pathways that connect what you think and feel to how your body responds to infections, inflammation, and cellular repair.



🟢 1. The thought → emotion → biology circuit

a) Thought and emotion

A thought (for example, anticipating a threat) activates neural networks in the prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and hippocampus.
This generates an emotion (fear, calm, anger, security).

b) Autonomous nervous system

Emotion activates one of two major systems:
    • Sympathetic (alert/stress)
    • Parasympathetic (rest/repair)

c) Neuroendocrine axis

Especially relevant is the HPA (hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal) axis:
    • Hypothalamus → CRH
    • Pituitary gland → ACTH
    • Adrenal glands → cortisol and adrenaline

d) Immune system

Immune cells (lymphocytes, macrophages, NK) have receptors for neurotransmitters and hormones.
That is to say: they literally listen to what the brain “says”.



🟢 2. What happens in the immune system according to the mental-emotional state?

Chronic stress/sustained negative emotions

Produces:
    • ↑ Prolonged cortisol
    • ↓ NK cell activity (anticancer and antiviral)
    • ↓ Antibody production
    • ↑ Chronic low-grade inflammation (IL-6, TNF-α)
    • ↓ Tissue repair capacity

👉 The immune system goes into survival mode, not healing mode.



States of calm, security and meaning

They produce:
    • Parasympathetic activation (vagus nerve)
    • Cortisol in physiological ranges
    • ↑ IgA (mucosal immunity)
    • ↑ NK Activity
    • ↓ Systemic inflammation
    • ↑ regeneration processes

👉 The body interprets that it is safe to invest energy in defending and repairing.



🟢 3. Practical examples of what WE SHOULD AVOID

🔸1. Constant mental rumination

Example:

“And if I get sick…”, “Something bad is surely going to happen,” “I never rest well.”

Neuroimmune impact:
    • Continuous activation of the amygdala
    • Cortisol microdischarges during the day
    • Persistent inflammation

Key: It's not the event, it's the mental repetition.



🔸2. Living in threat mode (hypercontrol, constant urgency)

Example:
    • Check your cell phone compulsively
    • Do not allow breaks
    • Eat quickly, without attention

Impact:
    • Suppression of the adaptive immune system
    • Impaired digestion and intestinal immunity (70% of the immune system)



🔸3. Inhibited emotions (unexpressed anger, sadness)

Example:
    • “Nothing happens” when it does happen
    • Chronically avoid conflict

Impact:
    • Physiological activation without shock
    • Increased risk of inflammation and autoimmune diseases (well documented in PNI)



🔸4. Identity based on illness or stress

Example:
    • “I am a stressed person”
    • “I'm always tired”

Impact:
    • The brain predicts that state
    • The immune system adapts to that narrative



🟢 4. Practical examples of what WE SHOULD PROMOTE

🔸1. Physiological safety states (not just “positive thinking”)

Practical examples:
    • Slow nasal breathing (4–6 breaths/min)
    • Exhalations longer than inhalations
    • Contact with nature

Effect:
    • Vagal activation
    • Direct signal to the immune system of “there is no danger”



🔸2. Thoughts with meaning and coherence

Not naïve optimism, but meaning.

Example:
    • “This is difficult, but I am managing it”
    • “My body knows how to adapt”

Effect:
    • Prefrontal activation
    • Inhibition of the amygdala
    • Lower cortisol release



🔸3. Gratitude and body appreciation (measurable)

Practical example:
    • Before going to sleep, recognize 2–3 things in your body that worked well

Evidence:
    • ↑ Salivary IgA
    • ↓ inflammatory markers
    • Improved sleep (key to immunity)



🔸4. Respected biological rhythms

Examples:
    • Sleep at regular times
    • Eat without screens
    • Exposure to sunlight in the morning

Impact:
    • Synchronization of the immune system with the circadian clock
    • More efficient immune response



🔸5. Mindful movement (no physical stress)

Example:
    • Walking, gentle mobility, moderate training

Effect:
    • Release of anti-inflammatory myokines
    • ↑ immune surveillance without stress



🟢 5. Key idea to integrate

The immune system does not distinguish between a real physical threat and a sustained psychological threat.
Responds to the dominant internal signal.

Therefore, repeated thoughts produce sustained emotions that in turn produce hormonal signals, which trigger an immune response adapted to that context.
